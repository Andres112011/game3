📜 How to Play / Reglas del Juego

Clear instructions in English and Spanish for classroom play.

🇺🇸

Rules in English

  1. 1
    Dynamic Attempts: Unlimited attempts until your 1st pair. Then, it resets to 12 attempts. From your 3rd pair onwards, it resets to 5. If it reaches 0, Game Over!
  2. 2
    3-Second Memorization: When you flip 2 cards, they stay face up for exactly 3 seconds before resolving. Memorize their positions!
  3. 3
    Mismatch Penalty: If cards don't match, you lose 1 attempt (except in Unlimited phase).
  4. 4
    Wildcards (Extra Turn / Lose a Pair): They execute immediately!
    • Under 3 Pairs found: The wildcard triggers, then hides and shuffles the board!
    • 3+ Pairs found: The wildcard triggers, but stays permanently face up (disabled).
  5. 5
    How to Win: Find all 10 pairs without your attempts hitting 0!
🇪🇸

Reglas en Español

  1. 1
    Intentos Dinámicos: Ilimitados hasta tu 1er par. Luego se reinician a 12. Desde tu 3er par en adelante, se limitan a 5. ¡Si llega a 0, pierdes!
  2. 2
    3 Segundos para Memorizar: Al voltear 2 cartas, permanecerán descubiertas 3 segundos antes de resolverse para que recuerdes dónde estaban.
  3. 3
    Penalización: Si las cartas no coinciden, pierdes 1 intento (excepto en fase ilimitada).
  4. 4
    Comodines (Turno Extra / Pierdes Pareja): ¡Se ejecutan al instante!
    • Con menos de 3 pares: Se ejecutan, se ocultan y ¡barajan el tablero!
    • Con 3 o más pares: Se ejecutan y permanecen descubiertos sin molestar más.
  5. 5
    Cómo Ganar: ¡Encuentra las 10 parejas sin que tus intentos lleguen a 0!

💡 Grammar Examples & Pronoun Types

Review how Personal, Possessive, and Object pronouns work in everyday situations before playing.

🟢 Possessive Example

“This is her bag.”

Explanation: The pronoun her indicates possession (it shows that the bag belongs to a female person).
🟠 Object Example

“I like him.”

Explanation: The pronoun him is an object pronoun (it receives the action of the verb “like”).
🟢 Possessive Example

“That is mine.”

Explanation: The pronoun mine replaces the object (e.g., my bag) and shows possession.
